A few weeks back I was asked the seemingly inoquisous question : 
> In the context of engineering, what do you think people who become genuinely exceptional are usually good at?

Here was my response. 


Before we start I must take the time to define what I mean by "exceptional engineer". To my understanding an exceptional engineer is measured by his ability to create highly complex systems by his own power. For a truly exceptional engineer, within his field of work, the question isn't one of ability but of time and resources. He has the ability to envision, design and maintain the architecture of a highly complex system in his mind as well as the technical ability to bring it to life. If an expertise is slightly outside of his current circle of competence, he can identify and acquire the necessary knowledge needed to complete his current endeavour.

By definition, the harsh truth is, most engineers are not exceptional and likely never will be. These exceptional individuals are the outlier events, the fringe minority. Most organizations, independently of their best efforts, will not cultivate a talent pool of exceptional engineers, though, some organizations either through the nature of the work they are doing or their selection processes will manage to have a higher occurrence of these exceptional individuals.
But, to your question, what actually qualifies one as an exceptional engineer, separating them from the merely very competent or very experienced?

In truth, I have both only, and already, met 4 engineers I could qualify as truly exceptional and worked within a professional context closely alongside 2 of them.

These are people with inherently higher intellectual capabilities. 
Yet, calling them "very smart" would be too imprecise, rather, they had a much higher than average processing speed and ability to easily manipulate highly abstract and complex concepts. They had a fundamental speed advantage when reasoning about complex systems: everything took less time. 

Next, they tended to have much better than average long term memory, allowing them to recall specifics of concepts and system details they had encountered.

We could call these people "gifted", but having also met a large pool of people  equally if not much more gifted, that I cannot qualify as exceptional engineers and that seemingly would never attain the label, I can confidently say there is more than raw processing speed and memory. Do not misunderstand me, most of these gifted individuals sat comfortably as solid contributors that will grow with experience, but they were visibly not following the trajectory of becoming exceptional engineers. There is a deeply different trajectory followed that has elevated these individuals from merely gifted individuals to exceptional engineers.

Saying they "put in the work" is much too simple of a statement and doesn't do justice to the distinction. To them their field isn't simply a job, it is a part of their identity and self worth. Being good at their "work" is akin to becoming a better individual, both are intertwined. 

Another unique characteristic is that these engineers have independently built up a conceptual model of the systems they interact with. Most engineers will take a model that is given to them, starting from their education with the details filled in through their work. They are not looking to re-build their own internal representations from first principles, and the holes and logical incompatibilities in their model are not a problem but a feature of its construction. The model is a tool used towards their work and they will fill it in with details as their work requires, but build no further.

Exceptional engineers build their own model from first principles, because of this their models are much more nuanced, detailed and logically coherent. This comes at the cost of a slower initial ramp: it is faster to memorise concepts than to be able to understand them from first principles. Because of this, initially, these exceptional engineers might seem slower due to the much higher upfront investment needed. But, given that their model is built independently, unlike other engineers, it is also capable of growing much further and encompassing new concepts from which there is no readily available mentor or source of information. This extends to building truly novel ideas and forming unique insights.
